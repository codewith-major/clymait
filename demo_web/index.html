      How the numbers were made

      Data

      • CHIRPS v3.0 – monthly rainfall, used for yearly rainfall, dry months and rainfall variability.
      • NASA POWER – temperature and heat days.
      • SPEIbase 2.11 – drought history (October 6-month index, drought = −1 or lower).
      • FAO GAEZ v5 – crop suitability ratings (rainfed, low input, 2001–2020), used as the reference the models learn from.
      • FAOSTAT national yields were examined but not used: they did not track climate closely enough.

      Models (tested on squares the model had not seen)

      R² is the share of variation explained (1.0 would be perfect). Average error is in suitability points.

        About Clymait

        A climate and crop-rotation decision aid for Nigerian farmers, built for the Veritas AI hackathon (Climate track).

        The problem

        Rainfall in Nigeria is shifting and uneven, and a wrong crop choice costs a whole season. Farmers know their land, but they rarely see climate risk, crop fit and rotation effects side by side, and many have slow or no internet.

        What Clymait does

        • Explore: climate suitability for rice, cassava and yam across map squares, with a rainfall what-if slider.
        • Measure risk: drought history, dry spells and heat for states, from observed data.
        • Plan rotations: compare the current rotation with at least three alternatives on ten criteria, with a plain-language explanation.
        • Learn from farmers: save plans, give feedback and record what actually happened.

        Data

        • CHIRPS v3.0: monthly rainfall.
        • NASA POWER: temperature and heat days.
        • SPEIbase 2.11: drought history.
        • FAO GAEZ v5: crop suitability ratings the models learn from (rainfed, low input, 2001–2020).
        • Rotation rules: draft guidance from FAO, IITA, ICRISAT and Nigerian trials, each with its source and how much of it was read.

        Tested, and what failed

        • Suitability models were tested on map squares they had not seen (R² ). A simple rainfall curve did as well as complex models, so the simple one ships.
        • A drought-year predictor was built and not shipped: on future years it was no better than chance. The risk shown is what was measured in the past.
        • National FAOSTAT yields were dropped as a training signal: they did not track climate.
        • One unexplained result is flagged, not hidden: recent drought in a few states looks unusually high.

        Honest limits

          Built for low connectivity and privacy

          Everything runs in the browser from files saved on the device. It works offline after the first visit and can be installed on a phone. Field profiles, plans and feedback stay on the device; nothing is sent to a server. Use the download buttons to keep a backup.

          Next

          • Monthly rainfall, so planting windows can be checked.
          • Soil and terrain data (SoilGrids), the biggest gap today.
          • Agronomist review of the rotation rules, then yield and price data for real income estimates.
          • Future climate (NEX-GDDP) and farmers sharing solutions within a region.
